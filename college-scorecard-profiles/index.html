What the College Scorecard says about 1,935 US colleges: admission rates, net price and earnings

By George Li

The U.S. Department of Education's College Scorecard covers the whole range of US colleges, not only the selective ones. This post uses the 2026-10-15 release, compiled for 1,935 currently operating colleges whose main degree is the bachelor's, and reads the table as a whole. It reports what the median school looks like. Every figure below is the Scorecard's own, and a blank in the file means the Scorecard reports no value.

Most colleges admit most applicants

Of the 1,935 colleges, 1,579 report an admission rate. The median is 77.3 percent. Among them, 870 colleges, 55.1 percent, admit three quarters of applicants or more. Only 34 admit under 10 percent, 2.2 percent of the colleges, and 90 admit under 25 percent.

Each cell is the median school's figure among the colleges in that row that report it, not a weighted average of students. The median for-profit college admits 100 percent of applicants. Public and private nonprofit colleges have almost the same median earnings: $59,509 and $59,098. The for-profit median is $44,016.

What the sticker says, and what families pay

The median cost of attendance is $25,632 at public colleges, $51,000 at private nonprofit colleges and $39,458 at for-profit colleges. The Scorecard also reports an average net price by household income. Here is the median school's net price for the lowest and the highest income band:

Net price is lower for the lowest-income band than for the highest at 1,484 of the 1,527 colleges that report both, 97.2 percent. The median gap between the two bands is $9,794. At private nonprofit colleges the median net price for the lowest-income band is $18,539, well below the median cost of attendance of $51,000.

Price and pay do not move together

The sticker price differs by far more than the earnings do. The median private nonprofit college costs 2.0 times the median public college in cost of attendance: $51,000 against $25,632. Median earnings ten years after entry differ by $411 between the two groups. The selective tail is small, too: 90 colleges, 5.7 percent of those reporting, admit under a quarter of applicants.

Tests

A test requirement is reported by 1,591 colleges. Of those, 82 (5.2 percent) require tests, 1,022 consider scores but do not require them, and 487 say they neither require nor recommend them. No policy is reported by 344 colleges. Most colleges in the table do not require a test.

How to read the table

  • The unit is the college. A median school is not the typical student.
  • Earnings and completion measure past cohorts, not the class now enrolling.
  • The 25th and 75th percentile test columns bound the middle half of the enrolled students the Scorecard counts. They are not cutoffs, and section percentiles are not added together.
  • A blank is a value the Scorecard does not report. It is never zero.
  • The Scorecard is a U.S. government work. The table here is compiled, renamed and filtered from it, and nothing is added to the figures.
