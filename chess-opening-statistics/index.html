Which chess openings score best? Results from 1,619,715 Lichess games

By George Li

Chess players argue about which openings are "best". A better question is what actually happens when ordinary players choose them. On 1 September 2026, 1,619,715 rated Lichess games in blitz, rapid and classical fit a simple filter: both players within 100 rating points of each other, a normal ending or a flag, and a named opening. The results are tallied for 117 opening families and 647 named openings.

White's edge is small, and draws are rare

Across all 1,619,715 games, White won 49.7 percent, Black won 46.0 percent and 4.3 percent were draws. Counting a draw as half a point, White scored 51.9 percent. That is the baseline. An opening scoring far from 51.9 for White is doing something different from the average game.

How the filter works

A game counts only when the two players' ratings differ by at most 100 points and it ended in a result or on time, so lopsided mismatches do not drown out the opening. Each game goes into a rating band by the lower of the two ratings. A game is counted once under its full opening name and once under its family, the text before the first colon, which is why the table above lists families and the data file lists both levels.

Almost every common opening favors White a little

Take the 22 opening families with at least 20,000 games. In 21 of them White scores above 50 percent. The best for White is the Scotch Game, at 54.8 percent over 38,672 games. The lowest White score among the 22 is the Van't Kruijs Opening, at 50.0 percent over 21,108 games.

So choosing a "Black-friendly" opening as Black means choosing one that holds White to about even. No large family reverses White's first-move edge.

The draw rate rises from 3.8 percent to 6.1 percent, a rise of 2.3 points, while White's score barely moves. Stronger players draw more often, and they do not hand White a bigger edge.

Individual openings move with rating too. In the Sicilian Defense, White's score climbs with rating:

Below 1200 Black scores slightly better than White in the Sicilian. Above 2000, White does. Whatever the reason, a result for an opening at one rating does not carry to another, which is why the data keeps the bands apart.

What to keep in mind

  • This is one day. A rarely played opening has a wide margin of error.
  • The players who choose an opening are part of its result. A strong group choosing a line will lift its score.
  • The score is not an engine evaluation.
  • Lichess ratings are their own scale, and the data has no federation breakdown.
  • Only families with at least 200 games and named openings with at least 300 games are listed, so the family rows cover 1,617,978 of the 1,619,715 games.

The games come from the Lichess open database (CC0). The compiled tables, split by rating band and time control, are free to reuse under CC BY 4.0.
