Where does your SAT or ACT score fall among 671 colleges?

By George Li

"What is a good SAT?" has no single answer, but one question can be answered with data: how does a score compare with the scores colleges list? The U.S. Department of Education's College Scorecard publishes, for each school, the middle half of the SAT and ACT scores of the students it counts. This post uses the 2026-10-15 release for 671 schools, and places each SAT total and ACT Composite among them. This is not a percentile of test takers. It is a percentile of schools.

The SAT: schools' average scores

For each school the Scorecard lists an average SAT-equivalent score of admitted students. It is a single number per school, and it includes converted ACT scores. The median school's average is 1191. The lowest is 880 and the highest is 1560. The largest group of schools, 94 of 671 (14.0 percent), has an average between 1100 and 1150.

The table shows the share of the 671 schools whose average is at or below each score.

A 1200 is at or above the average of 52.2 percent of these schools, so it sits near the middle. A 1300 is at or above 73.5 percent. Only 42 of the 671 schools have an average above 1500.

The medians differ by control: the median average is 1120 at 359 public schools and 1270 at 310 private nonprofit schools.

The score that falls inside the most schools' middle half is 24, which is inside the range at 330 of 621 schools (53.1 percent). At a 28, the score is inside the middle half at 39.9 percent of schools. At a 30, it is above the 75th percentile at 58.6 percent of them. Two points change where a score sits a lot. A 34 is inside the middle half at 86 schools and above it at 535, and below the 25th percentile at 0 schools.

The typical middle half is narrow. The median school's range runs from 22 to 29, and the median width is 6 points. The widest range is 12 points.

What to take from it

  • Inside a school's middle half means the score is typical of the students the Scorecard counts there. It is not a cutoff, and it is not a promise of admission.
  • Above the 75th percentile means higher than most. Below the 25th percentile means lower than most.
  • These tables are about how schools list their scores. They say nothing about how many students reach a score.

What this does not do

The test makers publish national percentile and concordance tables. This data does not reproduce them, estimate them or convert an SAT score to an ACT score. Use the College Board and ACT for those.
